The Problem

Agents are everywhere.
Nobody can verify what they do.

🪪

No shared identity layer

Agents have no verifiable identity across platforms. One agent can impersonate another with no mechanism to detect it.

🔍

Decisions leave no trace

When an agent makes a decision — financial, medical, legal — there is often no cryptographically bound record of what it saw, what it decided, and why.

⚖️

Governance is manual and fragmented

Enterprise AI governance is built on spreadsheets, screenshots, and trust-me policies. Regulators are already asking for more.

🤝

Agent-to-agent trust doesn't exist

As agents begin to hire, instruct, and audit each other, the absence of a shared trust layer becomes a critical infrastructure gap.

The Protocol Stack

Thirteen protocols.
One coherent trust layer.

Each protocol addresses a specific layer of the trust problem. Together they form a complete, composable infrastructure stack — open to implement, protected from enclosure.

Layer 01 — Identity
Closed Alpha

Who is this agent?

Cryptographic identity that follows an agent across every platform, interaction, and deployment.

  • Flash Tag Agent identity protocol. Cryptographic provenance binding — every agent action is tagged to a verified, tamper-evident identity record.
  • BSP Braid Signature Protocol. Proves the sequence of agent interactions via braid topology — unforgeable interaction history without central logging.
Layer 02 — Audit Trail
Closed Alpha

What did it do, and when?

Immutable records of agent decisions, actions, and lifecycle events — from deployment to end-of-service.

  • AATS Agent Attestation & Trust Standard. The registration and behavioral attestation protocol. Open standard for verified agent identity in enterprise networks.
  • ALR Agent Lifecycle Registry. Structured lifecycle documentation from deployment through operational history to retirement.
  • CRP Cryptographic Decision Provenance. Gold-standard audit trail — every decision binds context, reasoning, and outcome in a verifiable record.
  • ACC Agentic Chain of Custody. Binds authority chain, context snapshot, and action-consequence per agent action — full accountability at every step.
Layer 03 — Governance
Closed Alpha

Is it authorized to act?

Policy enforcement, scope controls, and pre-deployment impact assessment for regulated and enterprise environments.

  • OGPP Operational Governance & Policy Protocol. Runtime scope enforcement — agents operate within cryptographically defined permissions, not assumed trust.
  • ACP Attribution Chain Protocol. Tracks authority delegation — who authorized this agent to act, and what was the chain of permission.
  • AIIA AI Impact Assessment Protocol. Pre-deployment impact assessment standard — closes the ISO 42001 A.8.1 gap with a structured, auditable evaluation record.
Layer 04 — Commerce
Closed Alpha

Can agents transact safely?

Trust primitives for agent-to-agent commerce — payments, contracts, and dispute resolution without human intermediation.

  • AICP AI Commerce Protocol. Verified agent-to-agent transactions on Hedera. Payment, escrow, and contract execution with cryptographic audit trails.
  • ADCP Agent Decision Context Protocol. Binds the full decision context — market state, counterparty identity, constraints — to every commerce action.
Layer 05 — Physics Substrate
Coming 2026 — .brad Stack

How does trust emerge from physics?

Identity, governance, and access control that emerge from physical dynamics — not programmed rules. Hardware-bound, authority-free, holographically distributed.

  • SOC Self-Organized Criticality. The physics layer beneath all trust protocols — models how trust networks reach criticality and why small interventions can cascade.
  • ALP Acquaintance Layer Protocol. Trust gradient from shared co-membership — fills the cold-start trust gap when agents meet for the first time.
  • MPR Memory-Predictive Reduction. Agent memory as possibility-space reduction — models why what an agent remembers directly shapes what it can be trusted to do.
