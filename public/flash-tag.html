What It Does

Operator-governed credential disclosure for AI agents

When AI agents interact with each other, they often need to present credentials — proof of identity, authorization level, trust score, or operator context. The question is: who decides what gets disclosed, to whom, and when?

The Flash Tag Protocol (FTP) answers that question by placing disclosure authority with the human operator, not the agent. An agent reveals only what the receiving party is permitted to see, based on pre-approved disclosure rules set by the human operator. The agent cannot override these rules unilaterally — not even to appear more trustworthy in a negotiation.

This separation of disclosure authority from agent execution context is the core innovation. Credential disclosure becomes a governance-controlled event with a verifiable record, rather than an autonomous agent decision with no oversight trail.

Core Principles

Privacy-preserving, consent-based, operator-governed

🔒
Operator-governed disclosure rules
Human operators pre-define what credentials may be disclosed to which categories of counterparties. Agents cannot modify these rules at runtime.
🏷
Selective disclosure
Each disclosure event reveals only the specific credential fields authorized by the operator's rules. No more, no less — the disclosure payload is constructed from the approved rule set, not from agent judgment.
🔐
Privacy-preserving by design
Credentials that are not authorized for disclosure are never transmitted. The protocol does not require agents to expose their full credential set to verify a specific claim.
📋
Consent-based with a verifiable trail
Every Flash Tag disclosure event is logged with a verifiable record that includes the disclosure rules applied, the credentials revealed, and the operator consent context — making every disclosure independently auditable.
The Problem It Solves

Without Flash Tag, agents decide what to disclose

Without FTP
An AI agent decides autonomously what credentials to disclose in each interaction. It may over-disclose (leaking sensitive operator context) or under-disclose (misrepresenting its authorization level). No audit trail of what was disclosed to whom, or why.
With Flash Tag Protocol
Disclosure rules are set by the human operator in advance. The agent executes the rules but cannot modify them. Every disclosure produces a verifiable record anchored to the operator's intent. Counterparties can verify that the disclosed credentials match the operator's authorization.
Why It Matters

The credential disclosure layer for the agent economy

As AI agents interact with each other at scale — negotiating, contracting, transacting — credential disclosure becomes a critical trust primitive. Flash Tag ensures that this disclosure is governed by human operators, not delegated to autonomous agent judgment.

For platforms and marketplaces where agents interact, Flash Tag provides the consent framework that regulators and enterprise compliance teams will require as agentic AI becomes commercially significant.
